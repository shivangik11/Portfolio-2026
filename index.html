Turning identity verification data into actionable insights for administrators

At 1Kosmos, I redesigned the verification analytics experience so administrators can understand verification performance, spot recurring failure patterns and investigate individual sessions, without digging through raw session data by hand.

Role
Product thinking, strategy, design
Team
PM, engineering and me
Timeline
2 weeks, Q3 2026
Built for
Security administrators, enterprise HR
Deliverables
Spec, working prototype, interaction design

    The problem

    Analytics showed what happened, but not why

    Administrators could see verification outcomes. To act on them, they needed to go further and answer questions the dashboard couldn't:

    • Why are verifications failing?
    • Are certain signals contributing to failures?
    • Which documents or regions are performing poorly?
    • Are failures isolated sessions, or is the same user failing again and again?
    The redesign needed to uncover the failures, risk signals and behavioural patterns behind verification outcomes, and connect them directly to sessions for investigation.

    The design challenge

    Surface meaningful patterns without overwhelming administrators

    The challenge was to turn complex session-level data into analytics that surface real patterns, then let administrators move straight from spotting an issue to investigating the sessions behind it. The experience had to answer four questions.

    1. How are verifications performing?Understand success, failure, retry and completion trends.
    2. Where are users struggling?Identify recurring failure reasons and high-friction verification steps.
    3. Are there meaningful patterns?Surface patterns across documents, geography, repeated activity and risk signals.
    4. What is causing the issue?Drill from an aggregate insight directly into the relevant sessions to find the underlying cause.

    What drove the decisions

    Guiding administrators from outcomes to root causes

    The gap: the outcome was visible. The context wasn’t.

    Old dashboard

    What happened?

    A snapshot of verification outcomes
    Administrators could see the status breakdown, but explaining a pattern meant opening individual sessions and investigating by hand.

    New dashboard

    Three layers, one connected investigation

    01 · What happened?
    Start with performance KPIs and keep the familiar outcome breakdown.
    02 · Why did it happen?
    See the reasons behind failures and the signals worth investigating.
    03 · Who or what is driving it?
    Switch from sessions to unique users to reveal repeat failures, even when the latest attempt passes.

    Design decisions

    Three decisions that connect a pattern to its evidence

    Make every failure explainable

    We added 14 error codes to the failure taxonomy. A generic “failed” outcome now comes with a specific reason administrators can understand, compare and investigate.

    Look beyond sessions to understand users

    We introduced two views of the same verification data: all sessions, and unique users. Repeated attempts become visible, instead of every session being treated as a separate person.

    Turn analytics into an investigation entry point

    Failure reasons are selectable and take administrators straight to the matching sessions, already filtered. Going back to Analytics keeps their previous filters and selections.

    That means administrators can move from spotting a pattern to examining the evidence without rebuilding their analysis.

    Administrators can now go from spotting a failure pattern to examining the sessions behind it, in one connected flow.

    From more data to clearer decisions

    Redesigning an identity verification results dashboard to help administrators understand outcomes and investigate failures.

    Role
    Product thinking, strategy, design
    Team
    PM, engineering and me
    Timeline
    3 weeks, Q3 2026
    Built for
    Security administrators, enterprise HR
    Deliverables
    Spec, working prototype, interaction design

      The context

      What happened, and what needs my attention?

      The results dashboard is where administrators review a user’s identity verification attempt. It needs to answer those two questions quickly.

      The challenge

      More visibility created more complexity

      Administrators needed more evidence to investigate failures. Shown all at once, every new detail competed for attention with the answer they came for.

      The questions

      Start with the questions administrators need answered

      Each level of review asks a different question and needs different information.

      1. Level 1

        Understand the outcome

        Did this attempt pass or fail, and why?

        Information neededVerification status, failure reasons, flagged checks and risk signals

      2. Level 2

        Investigate the session

        What happened during this attempt?

        Information neededChecks, signals, the verification journey, extracted data and captured images

      3. Level 3

        Understand the user

        How does this attempt relate to their previous activity?

        Information neededUser identity, location context, earlier attempts and other sessions

      Design decisions

      Two decisions that put the next decision first

      Make the first view a useful summary

      The first view brings together what administrators need to get their bearings:

      • Whether verification passed or failed
      • Why it failed
      • Any flagged checks or risk signals
      • The user’s first and last name
      • Where the user is from
      • Previous attempts

      This overview establishes the outcome and its context before an administrator opens the detailed evidence.

      Prioritise information by its relevance to the administrator’s next decision.

      Separate the overview from the investigation

      The dashboard supports two stages: a first assessment, then a deeper investigation. Administrators get a concise understanding of the result on arrival, and open the relevant tab when they need more detail.

      This progressive disclosure cuts down how much information competes for attention at once, while keeping the detailed evidence one step away.

      Make complexity available at the point it becomes useful.

      Administrators see the outcome and its context first, and the evidence when they need it.

      Redesigning authentication around how people actually use it

      Reimagining the 1Kosmos mobile experience to make everyday authentication faster, more accessible and easier to navigate.

      Role
      Product thinking, strategy, design
      Team
      PM, engineering and me
      Timeline
      6 weeks, Q2 2025
      Built for
      Enterprise workforce, on the mobile authenticator app
      Deliverables
      Spec, working prototype, interaction design, card animation

        The problem

        The interface prioritized one authentication method. User behavior had evolved.

        QR scanning occupied the primary screen area of the app. The generated code sat behind a secondary button, so anyone who needed it had to go looking for it.

        The design challenge

        How might we make frequently used authentication methods equally discoverable while reducing the effort required to access them?

        The challenge was not to introduce new functionality. It was to rethink the information hierarchy around existing features so it supports how people's needs had changed.

        What drove the decisions

        Make the code easy to find

        1. 01 · Usage data

          Review authentication methods

          See which actions people actually use, from event logging.

        2. 02 · Behavioural insight

          Recognise the new pattern evolving

          A secondary action was gaining relevance.

        3. 03 · Design decision

          Change the hierarchy, support both paths

          Promote the generated code. Keep the code visible and the scanner accessible.

        Before and after

        From a scanner with a hidden code to a code with a scanner beside it

        Before

        QR leads the experience

        Dominant action
        QR scanning occupies the primary screen area.
        Hidden code
        A secondary button leads to the generated code.

        After

        The code becomes the hero

        Visible on arrival
        The code is immediately available on Home.
        Choice preserved
        QR scanning remains a clear alternative.

        No new functionality was added. The hierarchy changed, and the code is now there when the app opens.

        Visual orchestration workflows for identity verification

        Workflow Builder is a drag-and-drop, no-code tool that lets administrators build, brand and test identity verification journeys without technical support.

        Role
        Product thinking, strategy, design
        Team
        PM, engineering and me
        Timeline
        4 months, Q4 2025
        Built for
        Security administrators, enterprise HR
        Deliverables
        Spec, interactions, working prototype

          What triggered it

          The same request, two or three times a month

          Solutions and customer success kept bringing us the same kind of request. A customer needed a workflow with a slightly different combination of verification steps, branding and content.

          Each request pulled in product, design and engineering, and together they took up one to two weeks of every month. Customers then wanted to preview, test and refine their journeys, which added more rounds of changes.

          A need that regular was an opportunity to make workflow creation self-service.

          • 2–3workflow requests a month from internal teams
          • 1–2 weeksof every month spent on custom delivery
          • 3 teamspulled into each one: product, design, engineering

          The design challenge

          How could administrators build, troubleshoot, preview and test identity workflows without engineering support?

          A canvas alone would not answer that. The experience had to carry an administrator through the complete journey.

          1. Choose verification steps

          2. Configure screens

          3. Apply company branding

          4. Test the flow

          5. Understand the results

          The result

          Customers now configure supported workflows themselves

          Routine builds no longer depend on product and engineering. Workflow creation moved from repeated custom delivery to a reusable process: build, brand, preview, test and investigate.

          • 1–2 hoursto build a typical workflow
          • 3–4 hoursto build a complex workflow
          • Self-serviceconfigured with no engineering support

          Design process

          One scope at a time, one continuous feedback loop

          The system and architecture were already in place, so we focused on shaping the administrator experience. Each scope went through the same five steps while engineering built the one before it.

          1. 01

            Define scope

            Break the builder into focused, manageable parts.

            • Core: canvas, node library, node editor, configuration, branding, preview and test
            • Then: versions, results, more complex nodes

            OutputA core experience, then a plan to expand

          2. 02

            Explore and design

            Turn customer needs and industry patterns into an initial design.

            • Competitor analysis
            • Customer feedback
            • Solutions expectations

            OutputFirst design draft

          3. 03

            Align and validate

            Refine with the PM, then gather feedback from customer success and solutions.

            • Explore alternatives
            • Agree on interactions
            • Check customer fit

            OutputRefined design direction

          4. 04

            Specify and review

            Document behaviour and incorporate engineering feedback.

            • Spec walkthrough
            • Resolve constraints
            • Update design details

            OutputScope ready to develop

          5. 05

            Develop and repeat

            Engineering builds while design moves to the next scope.

            • Build the current scope
            • Design the next scope
            • Test the implementation

            OutputCore builder, then advanced capabilities

          What we built

          Four things that make it self-service

          Start from a flow that already works

          We brought established flows into the builder as prebuilt templates: KYC, KYE, 18+ age verification and higher-level verifications.

          For custom requirements, administrators start from scratch and compose a workflow from individual nodes.

          Brand it without writing code

          Administrators change primary and secondary colours, update screen text and upload a company logo, all through the interface.

          HTML and CSS editing supports deeper visual customisation, and a JavaScript node gives technical users extra flexibility.

          Keep track of every version

          As administrators tested and refined workflows, they needed to tell iterations apart and see what had changed.

          Version management makes ongoing iteration traceable, giving teams clearer context for reviewing changes and investigating issues.

          Preview and test where you build

          Customers need to experience a workflow before deciding it is right. Administrators preview the journey, test it and go straight back to make changes, all in the same workspace. That cuts handoffs for routine adjustments.

          Flexible testing options let them share a workflow for feedback and try it across device types.

          Workflow creation moved from repeated custom delivery to a reusable process that customers run themselves.
